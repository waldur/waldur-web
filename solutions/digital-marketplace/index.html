Digital Marketplace

Overview

A digital marketplace lets public bodies buy IT services from vetted providers through one catalog, with ordering, provisioning and invoicing done online instead of a separate procurement for every purchase. In Waldur, providers publish offerings with their price plans, the operator decides who may publish and what goes on sale, and public bodies order within the rules the operator sets.

  • Providers publish their own offerings, with descriptions, price plans and terms of service.
  • The operator stays in control: it admits providers, and can require that new offerings are activated by its staff before they can be ordered.
  • Organization onboarding with checks against business registries in supported countries, and manual review elsewhere.
  • Ordering with approval by the buying organization's managers.
  • Fixed and usage-based pricing, with monthly invoices per buying organization.
  • Spending visibility: usage and costs per public body, provider and service.

In the portal

Recorded on a demo deployment: the organizations, people and numbers are made up.

Scenarios

The scenarios below can be combined in one marketplace.

Application store

The operator offers a list of validated IaaS, PaaS and SaaS offerings, supported and operated by a central IT authority.

Gateway for SMEs

The operator sets the rules of engagement and lets companies register and publish their products and services, with pricing. The operator checks the offerings before they are activated. In this scenario the marketplace can extend the tendering process.

Services between agencies and to businesses

Public agencies offer their own services to other agencies or to businesses through the same catalog, with ordering and accounting online.

Implementation

A marketplace depends on rules as much as on software, so the rollout adapts policies as it goes:
  • Start with a pilot marketplace to collect early feedback from buyers and providers.
  • In the pilot, publish at least two new applications, to test provisioning end to end.
  • Adapt marketplace policies, processes and requirements to what the pilot showed.
  • Run a closed beta with a larger group of providers and buyers.
  • Build up partners and local knowledge for wider adoption.
  • Base the production marketplace on open, published rules.

Project phases

  1. Requirements and planning

    Analyse the needs of buyers and providers, and write a plan for moving purchases online.

  2. Pilot marketplace

    Deploy a pilot with offerings under the operator's control.

  3. Adaptation of policies

    Adapt the policies for publishing and buying services where they stand in the way.

  4. Beta launch

    Open the marketplace to one category of providers, and tune the procedures.

  5. Knowledge development

    Train the operator's staff, buyers and providers, with hands-on sessions.

  6. Production launch

    Open the marketplace to all qualified providers.
