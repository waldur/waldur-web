HPC Center

Overview

HPC centers serve research groups, companies and national or European projects, each with an allocation of its own. Waldur gives them one portal for all of it: users request access or apply in a call, project leads manage their members, and the center sees usage per project. Slurm and MOAB clusters are connected through the Waldur Site Agent.

  • Allocations on Slurm and MOAB: accounts, limits and usage, kept in sync by the Waldur Site Agent, with GPU-hours on Slurm.
  • Calls for proposals with rounds and review, for centers that hand out time through calls.
  • Periodic limits on Slurm: carry-over of unused hours and fair-share decay from one period to the next.
  • Sign-in with eduGAIN, MyAccessID, Keycloak or LDAP, and accounts synchronized to FreeIPA or LDAP where the cluster needs them.
  • Web access to the cluster through Open OnDemand or FirecREST, with the same identities.
  • Paid access for companies, with price plans and invoices next to free academic allocations.
  • Usage reports per project, organization and offering.

In the portal

Recorded on a demo deployment: the organizations, people and numbers are made up.

How Waldur fits

The Waldur Site Agent runs at the center, next to the cluster. It creates accounts and sets limits for the projects ordered in Waldur, and reports their usage back. It only connects out to Waldur, so the cluster needs no inbound ports and its credentials stay on site. Private clouds such as OpenStack or OpenNebula, storage and other services sit in the same catalog.

What stays with other systems

  • Job scheduling, queues and fair-share stay with Slurm or MOAB. Waldur sets the limits they enforce.
  • Software environments and job submission stay on the cluster, reached through SSH, Open OnDemand or FirecREST.

Implementation

A rollout usually starts with one cluster and direct requests, and adds calls and federated sign-in.

Project phases

  1. Proof of concept

    Deploy Waldur and connect one Slurm cluster through the Waldur Site Agent. Create an offering with CPU- and GPU-hour limits.

  2. Identity

    Register Waldur in eduGAIN or connect MyAccessID. Synchronize accounts to FreeIPA or LDAP if the cluster needs them.

  3. Allocation process

    Decide how projects get time, through direct requests, calls for proposals or both, and set up periodic limits.

  4. Production launch

    Open the service to research groups. Set up usage reports, monitoring and backups.
