Government Cloud

Overview

A government cloud is run by a central IT authority for ministries and agencies. Waldur is the self-service layer of such a cloud: agencies order resources from a catalog, every resource belongs to an agency and a project, and the authority sees usage and costs per agency.

  • Self-service for agencies: virtual machines, Kubernetes and platform services ordered from a catalog, with approval by the agency or the IT authority.
  • Costs per agency: usage and costs per organization, project and service, for budgeting and chargeback.
  • National sign-in, such as TARA in Estonia, or any SAML or OpenID Connect identity provider, as well as LDAP and Microsoft Entra ID.
  • Several providers in one catalog: the authority's own cloud next to partner and commercial offerings, under the same rules.
  • Audit trail: events for orders, changes and access, for security reviews.
  • Open source: MIT-licensed and installed on the authority's own servers, without dependence on a single vendor.

In the portal

Recorded on a demo deployment: the organizations, people and numbers are made up.

How Waldur fits

The IT authority runs the infrastructure, for example OpenStack or VMware vCenter, and Waldur on top of it. Agencies are organizations in Waldur, their teams are projects, and every resource belongs to one of them. Clouds with an API are provisioned directly. Other services are offered as requests fulfilled by the authority’s staff or by partners.

What stays with other systems

  • Networking, firewalls, monitoring and backups of the infrastructure stay with the systems the authority already runs.
  • Procurement and contracts with partners happen outside Waldur. Waldur lists the agreed offerings and accounts for their use.

Implementation

A government cloud grows over years, so the rollout starts small and adapts policies as it goes:
  • Start with a pilot IaaS deployment to collect early feedback from agencies.
  • In the pilot, migrate at least two real applications, to test the migration process.
  • Adapt cloud policies, processes and requirements to what the pilot showed.
  • Launch production IaaS for existing workloads.
  • Add platform and software services, for applications built for the cloud.
  • Build up partners and local knowledge for further migrations.

Project phases

  1. Requirements and planning

    Analyse hardware, network and storage requirements. Write the high-level design and deployment plan.

  2. Pilot IaaS

    Deploy the first IaaS offering and migrate the first agencies. Train the operations team.

  3. Production IaaS

    Launch the government cloud into production, with monitoring and backups in place.

  4. Knowledge development

    Build a knowledge base for operators and agencies, with hands-on training.

  5. Ecosystem development

    Add platform and software offerings as agencies need them.
